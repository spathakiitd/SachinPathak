01 — Researcher Profile

About Dr. Sachin Pathak

Condensed matter physicist and materials scientist specializing in spin manipulation, magnetic interface physics, and thin-film heterostructures.

Dr. Sachin Pathak is an active researcher and faculty member in the School of Advanced Engineering (Applied Science) at UPES, Dehradun, having joined the institution in 2020. His scientific trajectory spans elite institutions in India and South Korea, where he has pursued fundamental experimental and computational inquiries into spin-dependent transport phenomena.

He earned his Ph.D. in Physics from the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Delhi, one of India’s premier technical institutions, where he established a strong foundation in nanomagnetism and thin-film deposition. Following his doctoral studies, he expanded his international research footprint in South Korea, serving as a Postdoctoral Researcher at Yonsei University and subsequently as a Research Professor at Korea University.

"By controlling spin degrees of freedom at nanoscale interfaces, we pave the way for computation that transcends conventional charge dissipation."

Dr. Pathak’s research centers on the synthesis, structural inversion asymmetry, and magnetic characterization of advanced thin films, superlattices, and magnetic tunnel junctions. His investigative work integrates beamtime experiments at international synchrotron radiation facilities, such as DESY (Germany) and Elettra (Italy), working in close synergy with global experimental consortia.

At UPES, Dr. Pathak is committed to fostering a collaborative research culture, supervising undergraduate, master’s, and doctoral dissertations while actively teaching foundational and advanced physics courses.

02 — Research Philosophy

Research Vision

"Engineering matter at the nanoscale to understand, control and exploit spin."

"Bridging fundamental condensed matter physics with transformative spintronic technologies."

Conventional microelectronics relies on moving electric charges, generating significant Joule heating and fundamental scaling limits. Spintronics harnesses the electron's intrinsic quantum angular momentum (spin) alongside its charge. Our vision focuses on uncovering the underlying interface physics, spin-orbit torques, and magnetic domain dynamics that will govern future ultra-low-power non-volatile memory, neuromorphic computing, and quantum sensor architectures.

SPINTRONICS CORE NEXUS
RESEARCH FOCUS

Spin-Orbit Torque (SOT)

Harnessing spin-orbit coupling phenomena, such as the Spin Hall effect and Rashba-Edelstein effect, to generate transverse spin currents capable of switching ferromagnetic layers with high speed and endurance.

Key Focus: Low-dissipation magnetization reversal • Ultra-fast switching

04 — Scientific Highlights

Selected Scientific Contributions

Featured case studies demonstrating research methodology, verified quantitative milestones, and insights into nanoscale magnetism.

NANOMAGNETISM ~8 nm PATTERNS

Ion-Induced Nanoscale Magnetic Pattern Formation

Research Question Can focused energetic ion irradiation induce localized chemical ordering or phase transformations at extreme sub-10 nm spatial resolutions without destructive material ablation?
Approach Carefully controlled ion beam irradiation combined with high-resolution microscopy and localized magnetic domain mapping.
Key Insight Demonstrated stable magnetic nanostructures down to approximately 8 nm feature size, verifying localized phase modification while preserving underlying matrix integrity.
Impact Provides a viable pathway for ultra-high-density patterned magnetic recording media and bit-patterned spin arrays beyond classical photolithographic limits.
SUPERLATTICES ORBITAL ANISOTROPY

Co/Pd Superlattices & Interfacial Spin-Orbit Coupling

Research Question How does structural inversion asymmetry at 3d/4d-5d ferromagnetic-heavy metal interfaces govern orbital magnetic moments and perpendicular magnetic anisotropy (PMA)?
Approach Synthesis of atomically abrupt Cobalt/Palladium (Co/Pd) superlattices followed by polarized magnetometry and interfacial structural analysis.
Key Insight Quantified the enhancement of orbital magnetic anisotropy stemming from hybridization between Co 3d and Pd 4d states across structural boundary interfaces.
Impact Informs design rules for high-thermal-stability perpendicular magnetic tunnel junctions and low-damping spin transport channels.
MAGNETIC SPECTROSCOPY MOKE & SYNCHROTRON

Dynamic Domain Wall Characterization & Depth Profiling

Research Question What is the precise depth-dependent spin polarization and domain wall pinning mechanism in layered spintronic heterostructures under external excitations?
Approach Integrated in-house Magneto-Optical Kerr Effect (MOKE) microscopy with synchrotron beamtime investigations at international facilities (DESY & Elettra).
Key Insight Disentangled bulk magnetic contributions from interface spin accumulations, pinpointing exact barrier roughness effects on coercivity.
Impact Validates analytical and micromagnetic models used for designing high-endurance spintronic read/write heads and memory devices.
