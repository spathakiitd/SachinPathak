01 — Researcher Profile

About Dr. Sachin Pathak

Condensed matter physicist and materials scientist specializing in spin manipulation, magnetic interface physics, and thin-film heterostructures.

Dr. Sachin Pathak is an Associate Professor of Physics in the School of Advanced Engineering (Department of Applied Science) at UPES, Dehradun. With nearly 13 years of academic and high-level post-doctoral research experience across India and South Korea, his career focuses on "Materials Engineering for Spintronic Devices."

He earned his Ph.D. in Physics from the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Delhi in 2014, working on magnetic materials for GHz frequency devices and custom polar MOKE instrumentation. He conducted 6 years of post-doctoral research in South Korea as a Postdoctoral Fellow at Yonsei University under the prestigious Brain Korea (BK21) Plus fellowship, and subsequently as a Research Professor at Korea University and Visiting Research Professor at Yonsei University.

"Materials Engineering for Spintronic Devices: Uncovering interfacial physics, spin-orbit torques, and domain dynamics to power zero-dissipation computation."

Dr. Pathak's work integrates thin film growth (sputtering, electrodeposition, thermal evaporation), advanced characterization (MOKE magnetometry, VSM, SQUID, synchrotron radiation spectroscopy), and micromagnetic simulations (OOMMF). At UPES, he leads research grants funded by UGC-DAE CSR and internal SEED funds, and mentors doctoral scholars in nanomagnetism and sensor physics.

02 — Research Philosophy

Research Vision

"Engineering matter at the nanoscale to understand, control and exploit spin."

"Bridging fundamental condensed matter physics with transformative spintronic technologies."

Conventional computing relies on charge currents, triggering inevitable Joule heating and quantum leakage limits. By leveraging the electron's spin alongside charge, our research pioneers spin-orbit torques, perpendicular magnetic anisotropy, and interfacial phase engineering to deliver ultra-low power non-volatile MRAM and brain-inspired neuromorphic computing.

SPINTRONICS CORE NEXUS
RESEARCH FOCUS

Spin-Orbit Torque (SOT)

Harnessing spin-orbit coupling phenomena, such as the Spin Hall effect and Rashba-Edelstein effect, to generate transverse spin currents capable of switching ferromagnetic layers with high speed and endurance.

Key Focus: Low-dissipation magnetization reversal • Ultra-fast switching

04 — Landmark Insights

Selected Scientific Contributions

Seminal discoveries documented in high-impact publications, patents, and synchrotron experiments.

ADVANCED SCIENCE (IF: 17.5) 0.23 meV/atom

Giant Orbital Anisotropy at Pseudomorphic Co/Pd Interfaces

Research Breakthrough Demonstrated colossal orbital magnetic anisotropy with strong spin-orbit coupling at atomically pseudomorphic interfaces of Co/Pd superlattices established through low-energy proton irradiation.
Significance Validated the direct role of 3d-4d/5d orbital hybridization in unlocking ultra-high perpendicular magnetic anisotropy without destructive lattice disorder.
APPLIED PHYSICS LETTERS ~10 nm PATTERNS

Non-Destructive Sub-10 nm Magnetic Patterning

Research Breakthrough Fabricated ordered 10 nm magnetic island arrays with 50 nm pitch (density up to 3 Tb/in²) via localized phase transformation induced by low-energy proton irradiation of paramagnetic Co3O4/Pd.
Significance Eliminated lateral cross-talk and planarization defects, circumventing the conventional limits of bit-patterned media.
NATURE SCI. REP. & APL 80% FASTER SWITCHING

Transient Pulse Current Switching for p-MTJ

Research Breakthrough Discovered that applying an overshooting transient (OST) pulse current during the write cycle of a perpendicular MTJ reduces switching latency by 80% and cuts power consumption by over 20%.
Patented Architecture Granted US Patent (US20180268889A1) and Korean Patent (KR20180104819A) for low-power magnetic memory switching.
